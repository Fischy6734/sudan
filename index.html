The Sudanese civil war primarily involves the Sudanese Armed Forces (SAF) and the Rapid Support Forces (RSF), with numerous smaller groups and international actors supporting each side. Main Domestic Combatants Sudanese Armed Forces (SAF): Led by General Abdel Fattah al-Burhan, the SAF is Sudan’s official military, including the Army, Navy, Air Force, and Republican Guard. It has roots in decades of authoritarian rule and is backed by factions of the Sudan Liberation Movement and the Justice and Equality Movement in certain regions Wikipedia Wikipedia +1 . Rapid Support Forces (RSF): Commanded by General Mohamed Hamdan Dagalo, known as Hemedti, the RSF evolved from the Janjaweed militias notorious for atrocities in Darfur. Initially aligned with the SAF, the RSF became a semi-autonomous paramilitary force and rebelled in 2023, controlling large parts of Darfur, Kordofan, and central Sudan Wikipedia Wikipedia +1 . Smaller Armed Groups: Other groups have joined the conflict, including the SPLM–North, which has attacked SAF positions in the south, and the Tamazuj movement, which has aligned with the RSF Wikipedia Wikipedia . Former rebel factions in Darfur and armed groups in eastern Sudan also support the RSF Wilson Center Wilson Center . International and Regional Involvement Support for SAF: Egypt and Turkey provide diplomatic and material support thesudantimes.com thesudantimes.com . Saudi Arabia is reported to back the SAF, hosting peace talks while supporting military operations Wilson Center Wilson Center . South Sudan and certain ethnic-based factions, such as the Beja tribes under Mussa Mohamed Ahmed, also support the SAF Wilson Center Wilson Center . Support for RSF: United Arab Emirates (UAE) has provided backing, with allegations of complicity in atrocities Middle East Eye Middle East Eye . Ethiopia and Eritrea support RSF paramilitaries, sometimes coordinating with RSF forces in regional conflicts Wilson Center Wilson Center . Chad allows RSF use of its territory for recruitment and logistics thesudantimes.com thesudantimes.com . Other Actors: Iran previously supplied weapons to the SAF, though this has ceased, and Kenya has allowed RSF political organization on its soil thesudantimes.com thesudantimes.com . Russia, China, Qatar, Algeria, and Libya have also been involved in various capacities, reflecting the war’s status as a proxy conflict with complex regional dynamics Wilson Center Wilson Center . Summary At its core, the Sudanese civil war is a confrontation between the SAF and RSF, with smaller domestic groups and a wide array of international actors influencing the conflict. The war has caused massive displacement, humanitarian crises, and ongoing atrocities, making it one of the deadliest and most complex conflicts in the world today thesudantimes.com thesudantimes.com +2 .
